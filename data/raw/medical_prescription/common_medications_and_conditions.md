# Common Medications and Medical Conditions Guide

## Overview
This guide provides evidence-based information about common medications and medical conditions. Always consult a qualified healthcare provider before starting, stopping, or changing any medication.

---

## Pain and Fever Medications

### Paracetamol (Acetaminophen)
**Brand Names**: Tylenol, Panadol, Calpol
**Uses**:
- Relieve mild to moderate pain (headaches, toothaches, muscle aches)
- Reduce fever
**Dosage**:
- Adults: 500-1000mg every 4-6 hours, maximum 4000mg per day
- Children: Based on weight, usually 10-15mg/kg every 4-6 hours
**Side Effects**:
- Rare at recommended doses
- Overdose can cause liver damage
**Warnings**:
- Do not take more than 4000mg in 24 hours
- Avoid alcohol while taking paracetamol
- Check other medications for paracetamol to avoid accidental overdose

### Ibuprofen
**Brand Names**: Advil, Motrin, Nurofen
**Uses**:
- Relieve pain and inflammation (arthritis, sprains, headaches)
- Reduce fever
**Dosage**:
- Adults: 200-400mg every 4-6 hours, maximum 1200mg per day
**Side Effects**:
- Stomach pain or indigestion
- Nausea
- Dizziness
**Warnings**:
- Take with food or milk to reduce stomach upset
- Long-term use can increase risk of heart attack or stroke
- Not recommended for people with stomach ulcers, kidney problems, or heart disease

### Aspirin
**Brand Names**: Bayer, Ecotrin
**Uses**:
- Relieve pain and inflammation
- Reduce fever
- Low-dose (75-100mg daily) for heart attack and stroke prevention in at-risk individuals
**Dosage**:
- Pain relief: 325-650mg every 4-6 hours
- Heart health: 75-100mg once daily (only as directed by doctor)
**Side Effects**:
- Stomach irritation
- Increased risk of bleeding
**Warnings**:
- Do not give to children or teenagers (risk of Reye's syndrome)
- Avoid with blood thinners unless directed by doctor

## Allergy Medications

### Cetirizine
**Brand Names**: Zyrtec, Reactine
**Uses**:
- Relieve hay fever symptoms (sneezing, runny nose, itchy eyes)
- Relieve allergic skin reactions (hives, itching)
**Dosage**:
- Adults and children over 12: 10mg once daily
- Children 6-12: 5-10mg once daily
**Side Effects**:
- Drowsiness (less than older antihistamines)
- Dry mouth
- Headache
**Warnings**:
- May cause drowsiness in some people; avoid driving until you know how it affects you
- Avoid alcohol

### Loratadine
**Brand Names**: Claritin, Alavert
**Uses**:
- Relieve hay fever symptoms
- Relieve hives and itching
**Dosage**:
- Adults and children over 6: 10mg once daily
**Side Effects**:
- Headache
- Dry mouth
- Fatigue
**Warnings**:
- Generally non-drowsy at recommended doses

## Heartburn and Acid Reflux Medications

### Omeprazole
**Brand Names**: Prilosec, Losec
**Uses**:
- Treat heartburn and acid reflux
- Treat and prevent stomach and duodenal ulcers
- Treat GERD (Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease)
**Dosage**:
- Heartburn: 20mg once daily for 4-8 weeks
- Maintenance: 20mg once daily or 10mg once daily
**Side Effects**:
- Headache
- Stomach pain
- Nausea
- Diarrhea
**Warnings**:
- Long-term use (over 1 year) may increase risk of bone fractures, C. difficile infection, or low magnesium
- Take 30-60 minutes before food

### Ranitidine
**Brand Names**: Zantac
**Uses**:
- Relieve heartburn and indigestion
- Treat and prevent ulcers
**Note**: Many brands have been withdrawn due to potential contamination
**Side Effects**:
- Headache
- Dizziness
- Constipation or diarrhea

## High Blood Pressure Medications

### Amlodipine
**Brand Names**: Norvasc
**Uses**:
- Treat high blood pressure (hypertension)
- Treat angina (chest pain)
**Dosage**:
- Adults: 5-10mg once daily
**Side Effects**:
- Swelling of ankles or feet
- Dizziness
- Headache
- Fatigue
**Warnings**:
- May cause dizziness when standing up quickly (orthostatic hypotension)

### Lisinopril
**Brand Names**: Zestril, Prinivil
**Uses**:
- Treat high blood pressure
- Treat heart failure
- Improve survival after heart attack
**Dosage**:
- Hypertension: 10-40mg once daily
**Side Effects**:
- Dry cough (common)
- Dizziness
- Headache
- High potassium levels
**Warnings**:
- Do not take if pregnant or breastfeeding
- May cause allergic reactions (rare but serious)

## Diabetes Medications

### Metformin
**Brand Names**: Glucophage, Glumetza
**Uses**:
- Treat type 2 diabetes
- First-line treatment for type 2 diabetes
**Dosage**:
- 500mg twice daily or 850mg once daily, gradually increased as needed
**Side Effects**:
- Stomach upset (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea)
- Metallic taste in mouth
**Warnings**:
- Take with food to reduce stomach upset
- Rare but serious risk of lactic acidosis (seek medical help immediately if you have muscle pain, trouble breathing, or unusual tiredness)

### Insulin
**Uses**:
- Treat type 1 diabetes
- Treat type 2 diabetes when other medications aren't enough
**Types**:
- Rapid-acting (insulin aspart, insulin lispro)
- Short-acting (regular insulin)
- Intermediate-acting (NPH insulin)
- Long-acting (insulin glargine, insulin detemir)
**Side Effects**:
- Low blood sugar (hypoglycemia) – symptoms include shaking, sweating, hunger, confusion, dizziness
- Injection site reactions
**Warnings**:
- Learn to recognize and treat low blood sugar immediately
- Always check blood sugar levels regularly
- Rotate injection sites

## Antibiotics

### Amoxicillin
**Brand Names**: Amoxil
**Uses**:
- Treat bacterial infections (ear infections, sinus infections, pneumonia, urinary tract infections)
**Dosage**:
- Adults: 250-500mg every 8 hours or 500-875mg every 12 hours
**Side Effects**:
- Nausea
- Vomiting
- Diarrhea
- Rash
**Warnings**:
- Complete the full course even if you feel better
- May reduce effectiveness of birth control pills
- Do not take if allergic to penicillin

### Azithromycin
**Brand Names**: Zithromax
**Uses**:
- Treat bacterial infections (chest infections, ear infections, skin infections)
- Single-dose option for some infections
**Dosage**:
- 500mg once daily for 3-5 days
**Side Effects**:
- Stomach upset
- Nausea
- Diarrhea
- Headache
**Warnings**:
- Take on empty stomach for better absorption
- May cause heart rhythm problems in rare cases

## Mental Health Medications

### Sertraline
**Brand Names**: Zoloft
**Uses**:
- Treat depression
- Treat anxiety disorders (GAD, OCD, panic disorder, PTSD)
- Treat social anxiety disorder
**Dosage**:
- Depression/anxiety: 50-200mg once daily
**Side Effects**:
- Nausea
- Headache
- Insomnia or drowsiness
- Sexual side effects
- Weight changes
**Warnings**:
- May take 4-6 weeks to feel full effect
- Do not stop suddenly (risk of withdrawal symptoms)
- Increased risk of suicidal thoughts in young adults (monitor closely)

### Fluoxetine
**Brand Names**: Prozac
**Uses**:
- Treat depression
- Treat OCD
- Treat bulimia nervosa
- Treat panic disorder
**Dosage**:
- Depression: 20-80mg once daily
**Side Effects**:
- Nausea
- Headache
- Insomnia
- Sexual side effects
**Warnings**:
- Same as sertraline

## Common Medical Conditions

### Fever
**What is it?**: Elevated body temperature, usually above 38°C (100.4°F)
**Causes**:
- Infections (viral or bacterial)
- Inflammatory conditions
- Vaccinations
**Home Care**:
- Stay hydrated
- Rest
- Paracetamol or ibuprofen (if appropriate) to reduce fever and discomfort
**When to see a doctor**:
- Fever in infants under 3 months
- Fever over 40°C (104°F)
- Fever lasting more than 3 days in children or 5 days in adults
- Symptoms like difficulty breathing, chest pain, confusion, stiff neck

### Headache
**Types**:
1. Tension-type headaches (most common)
2. Migraines
3. Cluster headaches (rare)
**Home Care**:
- Rest in dark, quiet room
- Paracetamol or ibuprofen
- Drink water (dehydration is common cause)
**When to see a doctor**:
- Sudden, severe headache ("worst ever")
- Headache with fever, stiff neck, confusion, weakness, vision changes
- Headache after head injury
- Frequent or worsening headaches

### Common Cold
**Causes**: Viral infection (rhinovirus most common)
**Symptoms**:
- Runny or stuffy nose
- Sore throat
- Cough
- Mild fever
- Fatigue
**Treatment**:
- No cure; symptoms usually last 7-10 days
- Rest
- Stay hydrated
- Paracetamol or ibuprofen for pain/fever
- Warm drinks and honey (for cough in children over 1)
**When to see a doctor**:
- Symptoms last more than 10 days
- Fever lasts more than 3 days
- Difficulty breathing
- Chest pain
- Severe sore throat with fever (possible strep throat)

### High Blood Pressure (Hypertension)
**What is it?**: Pressure in arteries is consistently too high
**Symptoms**: Often no symptoms ("silent killer")
**Complications if untreated**:
- Heart attack
- Stroke
- Kidney disease
- Eye problems
**Management**:
- Healthy diet (low salt, plenty of fruits/vegetables)
- Regular exercise
- Maintain healthy weight
- Reduce stress
- Medications (if lifestyle changes not enough)

### Type 2 Diabetes
**What is it?**: Body doesn't use insulin properly or doesn't make enough insulin
**Symptoms**:
- Increased thirst and hunger
- Frequent urination
- Fatigue
- Blurred vision
- Slow-healing sores
**Management**:
- Healthy diet
- Regular exercise
- Weight management
- Medications or insulin (as directed)
- Regular blood sugar monitoring

### Asthma
**What is it?**: Chronic condition affecting airways, causing wheezing, breathlessness, chest tightness
**Triggers**:
- Allergens (dust mites, pollen, pet dander)
- Air pollution
- Smoke
- Exercise
- Cold air
- Viral infections
**Management**:
- Avoid triggers
- Inhaled corticosteroids (preventer)
- Short-acting beta-agonists (reliever)
- Follow asthma action plan from doctor

## General Guidelines
- Always read and follow medication labels
- Keep medications out of reach of children
- Do not share medications with others
- Store medications as directed (some need refrigeration, some need to be kept in dry places)
- Keep a list of all medications you take (prescription and over-the-counter) to show doctors and pharmacists
- Tell your doctor about all medications, supplements, and herbs you take to avoid interactions

---

## When to Seek Emergency Medical Help
Call emergency services immediately if you experience:
- Chest pain or pressure
- Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath
- Sudden numbness or weakness of face, arm, or leg (especially on one side)
- Sudden confusion, trouble speaking or understanding
- Sudden trouble seeing in one or both eyes
- Sudden trouble walking, dizziness, loss of balance or coordination
- Severe or persistent vomiting or diarrhea
- Severe allergic reaction (hives, swelling of face/lips/tongue, difficulty breathing)
- Bleeding that doesn't stop after 10 minutes of direct pressure
- Loss of consciousness
